# Give clerks the journal they know

A clerk can already type a batch on the BOS Datasheet the way they type a journal in Business Central: **one row is one record**, Enter moves through the fields they fill, F8 copies the cell above, and a date word or a simple sum can be typed into that cell. The sheet still fails the habits that keep a long batch honest. The clerk cannot choose which columns stay on screen, which columns Enter skips, or which lines are wrong, and a typed work date is treated as today. Business Central keeps data entry on the keyboard, lets each person arrange the columns once, and shows mistakes before anyone posts. Those are the must-haves still to build. Grouping, recurring journals, and a workbook of tabs and cell addresses can wait, because they do not help the first real batch.

This report is for a product owner. It uses the words a clerk would use. It was drawn from Microsoft’s current Business Central guides (read on 16 October 2026), the older NAV Windows-client shortcut list where a habit still differs, partner and trainer write-ups, and a read of the Datasheet source. The component was read, not clicked through in a browser.

## The must-have list

Read the last column first. **Already there** means a clerk can do it on the Datasheet today. **Finish it** means the daily habit is still missing or only half built. Later ideas are in their own section, so this list stays the work that changes a clerk’s day.

A feature is must-have here when it changes the feel of entering lines, even if a batch could technically be posted without it. Microsoft’s own journal page puts the typing habits, the visible posting date, and the problem list on the entry path. Freeze and column layout sit in personalization rather than in the posting rules. They are still must-have for a wide journal, because a clerk who cannot see the account and the date will mis-key the line.

## Typing a batch is largely solved

The daily keystrokes Microsoft and trainers repeat are already on the sheet. Enter walks the fields marked for Quick Entry, Tab walks every editable column, and both continue onto the next row. F8 copies the cell above and refuses a locked cell. F4 and Alt+Down both open a choice list or a lookup, and typing narrows that list. A yes/no column is a checkbox. A number cell accepts `19+19` and stores the result. A date cell accepts `t`, `w`, a day number, month-end words, and a closing-date mark. Copy and paste write values into the cells the clerk is allowed to change. Search, a single-column sort, and dragging a column wider are in place. A blank row waits at the bottom, and the left gutter shows whether that row is new, dirty, or current. On a phone, each record becomes a card. Under an amount column, the sheet adds up the rows on screen, and the status line adds up whatever cells are highlighted.

That set matches the product’s own description of this component: the desktop journal and the studio record sheet, with a row gutter, Quick Entry, F8, frozen columns, and the same field editors used elsewhere ([Page design](file:///d:/Work%20Studio/Bos/docs/28_page_meta_object_design.md)). Frozen columns are the one item in that sentence that the clerk still cannot switch on. The sheet will freeze the left columns only when the page asks it to, and the default is none.

Two inherited keys are worth keeping on purpose. NAV clerks still reach for F4 to open a lookup. Current Business Central documents Alt+Down for that job and does not list F4. The Datasheet answers both, which is the right overlap. Enter is the other inherited argument. Microsoft’s shortcut page describes Enter twice: once as Quick Entry, and once as “open the card” when the row has a card ([Keyboard shortcuts](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/keyboard-shortcuts)). The page never says which meaning wins on a journal. The Datasheet already uses Enter for Quick Entry. Keep that. Opening the related record needs its own action, Ctrl+Alt+Down or choosing the number, so Enter stays the typing key.

The speed complaints that have a public source are not about scrolling a normal batch. One forum report describes about 77,000 general-journal lines taking days to post ([Large journal post](https://community.dynamics.com/forums/thread/details/?threadid=41e86cf6-c23a-f011-b4cc-000d3a990cc2)). Another describes the account field hesitating while approval workflows run ([Journal validation performance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5904672/365-business-central-issue-performance-issue-when)). The sheet already windows long lists in memory once there are 48 rows or more. Chasing a smoother scroll will not fix a slow post or a slow account check. Those belong with the posting rules.

## Six gaps still decide whether the batch feels safe

**The work date is the first gap, because it posts to the wrong day.** Business Central treats `w` as the company work date from My Settings, and a two-digit day uses that work date’s month and year. When that date is not today, the page says so ([Change basic settings](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-change-basic-settings), [Entering dates and times](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-enter-date-ranges)). The Datasheet’s date words are real, and the commit path calls them without the company work date, so `w` resolves to today. A clerk who thinks they are posting into last month’s open period will post into today. Connecting the company work date, and showing a quiet reminder when it is not today, is the smallest change with the largest effect.

**The next gap is the keys a clerk’s hands already know.** Alt+N inserts a line. Ctrl+Delete deletes a journal line. Page Up and Page Down move a screen at a time. F2 toggles “replace everything” against “add at the end.” The Datasheet has a permanent blank row, which is a reasonable place to type the next record, and it has arrows, Home, and End. It still clears a cell when the clerk presses Delete, and it still removes a row only from the right-click menu. A NAV or Business Central clerk will press Ctrl+Delete and expect the line to disappear. Give them that key, and give them Alt+N even though the blank row exists, so both habits land on a new record. While a cell is being edited, Left and Right should move inside the value and change column only at the ends, which is the editable-list rule ([Enter data](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-enter-data)).

**Column layout is the third gap, and it has to remember the person.** Business Central lets a clerk resize columns without a setup mode, then hides, shows, reorders, freezes, and sets Quick Entry inside personalization. That layout is personal. It is shared across that user’s ordinary views of the same list. It is not something they can hand to a colleague by copying a link ([Personalize your workspace](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-personalization-user), [List views FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-views-faq)). The Datasheet can resize for the current visit, and it can freeze if a programmer passes a column count. The clerk cannot hide Account Type, pin the account and the date, or exclude a defaulted column from Enter. Build those four choices, and store them for that clerk. A company-wide starting layout, set by an administrator the way Business Central profiles work, can follow later.

**Hiding columns must not invent a posting date.** This is the trap inside the layout work. On the general journal, fewer columns means every line uses the same posting date. A clerk who needs several dates has to show more columns ([Working with general journals](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-work-general-journals)). When the Datasheet gains hide-column, the posting date either stays visible or the page states, in plain words, that one date applies to every line. A hidden date that silently stamps today on the whole batch recreates the mistake Microsoft’s “show more columns” rule was written to prevent.

**Finding a line is the fifth gap.** Search across the visible values is already there, and so is sorting by one heading. What is missing is the gesture clerks use mid-batch: “show me only this customer,” which is Alt+F3, plus a range such as an amount band or a date interval ([Sort, search, and filter](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-enter-criteria-filters)). The current filter is “this text appears somewhere in the cell,” or a pick list for fixed choices. That is a useful start. It is not how a bookkeeper narrows a batch. Named views that survive until next month are a list-page feature. Journals are worksheets, and Microsoft omits the Save icon there ([List views FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-views-faq)). Save the filter for the visit. Do not block the first journal on named views.

**The sixth gap is seeing the bad lines, and seeing the current line’s context, before post.** Business Central checks a finance journal when the batch loads and again when the clerk moves lines. A FactBox lists issues for the current line and the whole batch. Show Lines with Issues hides the clean ones. A field can be drawn as needing attention when a rule is true ([Working with general journals](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-work-general-journals), [Style property](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/developer/properties/devenv-style-property)). The Datasheet can reject the cell being saved and show a banner. It cannot yet list every problem in the batch, filter to those rows, or let the page paint a value that fails a rule. The rules themselves (a missing description, a dimension, a tax setup) belong to the journal. The sheet’s job is to show the result and to let the clerk jump to the line.

The same move-to-another-line moment is when the side panel should update. In Business Central that panel carries the account or customer, the posting setup, the problems, and the links. Frequent dimensions are columns on the line, two global and up to eight shortcuts, so department and project are typed like any other field. The full set opens with Alt+D ([Work with dimensions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/finance-dimensions)). The Datasheet should keep doing what it already can: show whatever columns the page gives it. It should also tell the page which row is selected, so the panel beside the sheet can follow. The panel is not a second grid inside the cell. The product’s page design already puts related facts in that side area ([Page design](file:///d:/Work%20Studio/Bos/docs/28_page_meta_object_design.md)).

A required field needs its own mark. Business Central’s red asterisk means “posting will stop without this,” and the clerk may still leave the cell ([Enter data](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-enter-data)). The Datasheet’s star means “this row is new.” Those two meanings have to stay visually distinct, or a clerk will treat a mandatory amount as a new-line marker. Microsoft checks the value when the clerk leaves the field, and Alt+Up shows the reason. The banner is enough for a mouse user. The keyboard path still needs a way to read that reason without hunting for it. Microsoft never documents a key that skips a bad value, so the sheet should not invent one.

Calculated figures are a smaller, sharper version of the same idea. A balance the system computes should open the entries behind it ([DrillDown property](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/developer/properties/devenv-drilldown-property)). An amount the clerk just typed is already the record. The column total at the bottom of the sheet is a third thing again: it adds the rows on screen. Keep it. It is more than Business Central shows on the entry journal, and it helps a clerk see what they highlighted. It is not a substitute for drill-down, and it is not a reason to group the rows.

## The journal page still has to catch a bad post

Some must-haves are not widgets inside the grid. They are actions on the batch. The Datasheet’s job is to display their result: which lines are wrong, which date is in force, which amount was suggested. Leaving them off the backlog would ship a pleasant grid that still fails at post.

The clerk previews the entries posting would create, and nothing is committed. Microsoft offers that on every document and every journal that can be posted ([Preview posting](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-how-preview-post-results)). A richer preview that groups the ledger by account arrived later and can wait. The first preview only has to show the entries.

The clerk balances the line. If the batch has a default balancing account, it fills in when they enter the account. Otherwise they type both sides. A positive amount debits one and credits the other. Suggest Balancing Amount can fill the figure that ties lines sharing a document number ([Working with general journals](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-work-general-journals)). Both patterns are real. A partner walkthrough notes that some clerks prefer a second line over a balancing-account column, and on that pattern the second amount stays manual unless the suggestion is on ([Suggest Balancing Amount](https://yzhums.com/54917/)). The sheet does not need a special balancing widget. It needs the page to be allowed to put those fields on the row, and to write a suggested amount into the cell the clerk is about to accept.

Document numbers stay in series. If the batch uses a number series, numbers on lines or groups have to be sequential or posting fails. Renumber Document Numbers fixes them and keeps lines that share a number grouped ([Working with general journals](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-work-general-journals)). A 2025 write-up shows preview succeeding and post failing because the clerk typed a number the series did not allow ([Journal number series](https://thedynamicsexplorer.com/2025/11/10/dynamics-365-business-central-error-message-you-have-one-or-more-documents-that-must-be-posted-before-you-post-document-no-when-posting-a-journal/)). That failure belongs in the problem list on the sheet, not in a surprise at the end.

Links that give context to the transactions are copied onto the posted entries. Microsoft’s example is a receipt image for an auditor ([Working with general journals](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-work-general-journals)). Files on every item-journal line are a customization in the sources reviewed here, not a base habit ([Item journal attachments](https://dharmendra-d365.blogspot.com/2025/09/document-attachment-with-item-journal.html)). For the first journal, a link the posting run keeps is enough. A comment typed on the journal line is a poor place to store an audit note until someone confirms it survives posting: community replies disagree on that field ([Journal comment field](https://community.dynamics.com/forums/thread/details/?threadid=ce9811fe-03a3-f011-bbd3-7ced8d1e92e2)).

## What can wait until the first batch already posts

These are real Business Central, SAP, or Salesforce behaviors. They help a clerk who repeats the same entry every month, reviews a list, or updates many existing records. They are the wrong first build for this sheet.

| What can wait | Why it waits |
| --- | --- |
| Recurring journals, allocation percentages, and reversal dates | Microsoft documents them for rent, subscriptions, and accruals. A one-off batch does not need a recurring method on day one ([Working with general journals](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-work-general-journals)). The date cell can already accept a formula such as `1M` when a field is a date formula. The recurring product around that field can wait. |
| Save a standard journal and pull it back later | This removes retyping of a batch the clerk knows they will enter again. One partner prefers it to recurring journals. Either can wait ([Standard journals](https://yzhums.com/15496/)). |
| Copy a posted journal back with the signs flipped | That is a correction, after the first journal has already posted ([Working with general journals](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-work-general-journals)). |
| Named views saved for next month | Journals do not offer the Save icon. Lists do. This component is the journal and the studio record sheet. Customer-list browsing is a different grid in the product’s page design ([List views FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-views-faq)). |
| Sort by several columns, group rows, or pivot | On the entry page, sort is one column. Shift-click multi-sort, row groups, and pivot appear after the clerk enters analysis mode, which replaces the action bar and does not change the data ([Analyze list data](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/analysis-mode)). Give that a separate review screen later. |
| Change many selected rows to one value in a single step | Salesforce list views can write one value onto the ticked records ([Mass update from list views](https://www.forvismazars.us/forsights/2024/11/update-multiple-records-with-list-views-in-salesforce)). F8 already covers the new-line case, one cell at a time. Mass edit is useful later and still means one field on many records. |
| A lookup that searches by several attributes, ranges, and exclusions | SAP’s value-help dialog is a full search page ([Value help dialog](https://www.sap.com/design-system/fiori-design-web/v1-84/ui-elements/value-help-dialog/usage)). The must-have is already met: type, see matches, pick one, without leaving the row. Recently used values at the top of a Business Central lookup can wait with it ([Enter data](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-enter-data)). |
| Files and a comment thread on every line | Standard on some documents, a partner extension on item journals. The link that posting copies is the documented audit trail. |
| A mode that hides the rest of the page and fills the screen with lines | Ctrl+Shift+F12 does this for document lines such as a sales order ([Enter data](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-enter-data)). A journal is already the whole page. |
| A full undo history | Esc already cancels the edit in progress. Business Central’s shortcut guide does not make a multi-step undo a journal habit. |
| Page inspection for support staff | Ctrl+Alt+F1 opens table, field, and filter details for someone diagnosing a page ([Inspecting pages](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/developer/devenv-inspecting-pages)). Clerks do not need it to post. |
| An administrator’s shared column layout | The must-have is each clerk’s own layout. | Search that finds words in any order, and filters such as “my customers” | Modern list search and tokens such as `%mycustomers` are documented, and modern search is still marked as a preview that can change ([Sort, search, and filter](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-enter-criteria-filters)). The current search box is enough for a batch. |

Focus mode, freeze, and column width were called polish in one reading of the trainer material, on the grounds that they do not decide whether the journal posts. Freeze and the clerk’s own column choices are pulled forward into the must-have list anyway. A wide journal that hides the date, or that makes the clerk scroll to see the account on every line, fails the day’s work even when posting would succeed. Focus mode stays in the waiting list because the journal page is already the lines.

## Each row stays one record

Date words and sums already live inside the field they belong to. `w` is a date on that row. `19+19` is the quantity or amount on that row. F8 copies one cell’s value onto the next record. Paste does the same for several cells. The line above the grid already shows the name of the active field and the value in it, as a readout. The mouse can already copy a value downward onto the following records, and a run of numbers can continue a step such as 10, then 20, then 30. Those write values onto records.

Leave all of that as values on the record. The component does not need sheet tabs, cell addresses, or a formula that makes one row’s amount depend on another row’s address. Business Central’s date formulas are words stored in a field, at most 20 characters, calculated by the system (`1M`, `CM`, `1D+1M-1D` on a recurring frequency). They are not a formula the clerk maintains across a grid ([Entering dates and times](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-enter-date-ranges)). Microsoft’s quantity math is the same kind of thing: the expression is typed into that quantity, and the stored result is a number ([Enter data](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-enter-data)). Analysis mode, where sums, groups, and pivot live, is a screen the clerk enters and then leaves. It does not change the journal underneath ([Analyze list data](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/analysis-mode)).

A few limits in the sources should stay visible so the build does not over-claim. Microsoft’s shortcut page never says whether Up or Down saves the cell before moving, beyond the general rule that a value is checked when focus leaves it. It never says Enter on a journal prefers Quick Entry when a card also exists. The quantity-math examples name quantity fields, not the Amount column on a general journal; the Datasheet already runs the same arithmetic on every number column, which is a fair extension and should stay inside that cell. No fetched page says the cursor is trapped in a bad cell. The classic claim that posting used to stop at the first error comes from a search extract of a partner article whose body was not re-read, so the safer source is Microsoft’s own reason for the problem list: catch issues while the clerk is still on the batch ([Working with general journals](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-work-general-journals)). The Datasheet inventory is from source, not from a live session, so phone cards and the exact focus of Ctrl+C were not watched in a browser.

## Conclusion

Business Central splits a clerk’s morning into three jobs, and the Datasheet has finished most of the first. Typing the line is in place: Enter, Tab, F8, lookup, date words, in-cell sums, paste, search, and a total under the amounts. Arranging the columns for this one clerk, and showing the bad lines before post, are the jobs that will make the sheet feel like the product people already know.

Build the company work date first, because `w` meaning today will silently post a batch into the wrong period. Then add the insert and delete keys, and F2’s replace-versus-append behavior, so hands trained on NAV and Business Central land where they expect. Then let the clerk freeze, hide, show, reorder, and exclude columns from Enter, and store that layout for them alone, with the posting date staying visible whenever lines do not share one date. Then show the problem rows, mark required fields with a symbol that does not also mean “new row,” and tell the page which line is selected so the side panel can follow. Preview, balancing, and document numbers sit on the journal page and should light up those problem rows. Recurring journals, grouped analysis, and mass edit are the tools of a clerk who already posts every month. They are a later product. The entry sheet stays a list of records.
